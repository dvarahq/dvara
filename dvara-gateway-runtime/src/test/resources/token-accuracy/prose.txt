The quarterly review covered three areas: customer retention, the cost of serving each request, and the time it takes a new team to go live. Retention held steady at ninety-four percent, which the team attributed to faster support replies and clearer invoices. The cost of serving a request fell by eleven percent after the gateway began caching repeated prompts, although the saving varied widely between workspaces. Teams that ask the same questions every morning saved the most; teams that write long, unique prompts saw almost no change.

Onboarding remains the slowest part. A new team still needs about two weeks to connect its first provider, agree on budgets and set up its policies. Most of that time is spent waiting for approvals rather than doing technical work. The proposal is to ship a starter policy pack and a default budget, so that a team can begin with safe limits on its first day and tighten them later.

Finally, the review noted that estimated token counts differ from what providers bill, especially for long documents and for languages other than English. Before changing any limits, the team wants a measured comparison across the providers it uses most.
